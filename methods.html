Disclosure timesCompany statements with sources and confidence (data/manual/disclosure_times.csv).

2. Launch event identification

Launch event identification
UniverseEvery orbital launch attempt in GCAT, 2000 to 2026.
FailureGCAT success score below 0.75 (GCAT's own pass or fail line). Partial failures below that line count as failures.
Pad explosionsExplosions before launch are recorded separately and are not launch failures.
Link to firmsEach failure is linked by hand, point in time, to its maker if listed and to listed rivals (section 4).

3. Sample construction

The counts measure different units. Firm-event reactions are not independent events.

Sample definitions
Historical launch failures138 orbital launch attempts, 2000 to 2026, with a GCAT success score below 0.75. Pad explosions before launch are kept separately.
Failures in the study27 failures with a listed maker or at least one listed Tier 1 or 2 rival.
Listed-maker cases4 failures whose rocket was built by a listed company (Avio SpA, Rocket Lab). Reported one by one as case studies.
Failures with listed rivals24 failures with at least one listed rival that has a usable price history.
Rival reactions (firm-events)42: one per listed rival per failure. The unit of each CAR, not an independent observation.
Independent events (clusters)22: failures that share a firm within 5 trading days form one cluster. The unit of inference.
Distinct listed rivals3: Avio SpA, Firefly Aerospace, Rocket Lab.
Failures not in the study, by reason (a failure can have several)
maker not a listed company97
no listed maker or listed competitor13
maker not listed, or not the owner, on that date1

4. Listed-company selection

Firm classification
MakerThe company that built the failed rocket, if listed at the time of the failure.
Tier 1 rivalListed; launch is a pure-play or major business; sells a rocket of the same size class to the same customer market; has flown that rocket in the previous 730 days.
Tier 2 rivalListed and close, but missing one Tier 1 condition (recorded per row in the firm map). Enters the test with Tier 1.
Tier 3Diversified aerospace groups where launch is a minor business. Descriptive only, never in the test.
Point in timeEach link uses listing dates, ownership and exposure as they were on the failure date (data/manual/firm_map.csv, data/manual/vehicle_segments.csv). Prices from before a firm's real listing (a pre-merger shell company) are dropped.

5. Event timing

88% of rival reactions (37 of 42) are dated by the launch-time proxy, not by a separately verified company statement.

ClassWhat it meansRival reactionsRival failuresMaker cases
Independently verified public disclosureCompany statement with a documented time320
Launch-time proxyGCAT launch time, used only where day 0 is insensitive to a small timing error37214
Uncertain or incomplete timingCompany statement whose time or date is not certain210
Total42244
How events are assigned to trading days
Assignment to a trading dayDay 0 is the first trading session at or after the moment the failure became public, on each stock's own exchange calendar (holidays and half days included).
Overnight and after-hours eventsA failure that becomes public after the close, overnight or on a non-trading day is assigned to the next session of that exchange. The same failure can therefore fall on different calendar days for different rivals.
When the launch-time proxy is allowedOnly where day 0 would not change if the true disclosure came 10 minutes earlier or up to 180 minutes later. Otherwise a sourced time is required.
Missing timesNo disclosure time is invented. A failure without a usable time does not enter the test.
SensitivityCheck R5 drops or shifts the uncertain disclosure times.

6. Market-return model

Market-return model
BenchmarkEach firm's home market index.
ModelMarket model by OLS: stock return = alpha + beta × market return + error, on trading days -250 to -30 before day 0.
Minimum historyAt least 100 returns. With 30 to 99 returns, a market-adjusted model (alpha 0, beta 1) is used and flagged. With fewer, the firm-event is dropped.
ReturnsSimple daily returns from adjusted closes.

7. Abnormal returns

Abnormal returns
DefinitionAbnormal return = actual return minus the market-model prediction for that day: the part of the move the market does not explain.
UncertaintyThe variance of each abnormal return includes the prediction error of the estimated model, not only the residual variance.

8. Cumulative abnormal returns

Cumulative abnormal returns and windows
DefinitionCumulative abnormal return (CAR) = the sum of daily abnormal returns over the event window.
Main windowT0 to T+1: the confirmatory test (H2).
Wider windowT−1 to T+3: robustness check R4.
One-month windowT0 to T+20: description only, no test.
CAR varianceWindow length, estimation length and the market's moves in the window enter the prediction-error variance (paper, equation 3).

Equations: paper, section 5.

9. Statistical inference

Tests and estimands
Prespecified testStandardized test of Boehmer, Musumeci and Poulsen (1991), with the Kolari and Pynnönen (2010) correction for correlation between rivals reacting to the same failure, applied within clusters. Null: mean standardized CAR is zero. p from a t distribution with clusters minus one degrees of freedom. Result: p = 0.40.
Mean testCluster-robust test of the plain equal-weighted average CAR (the published estimate, +0.28%, 95% interval −1.62% to +2.18%), p = 0.76, with a wild cluster bootstrap as a check.
Why two p-valuesThe standardized test gives volatile stocks less weight; the mean test weights every reaction equally. They test different quantities, so their p-values are not interchangeable. Each is reported next to the quantity it tests.
WeightingAverages that weight every event, every firm, or calmer stocks more answer different questions. When one firm has many reactions, weighting firms equally can change the sign. These are sensitivity analyses added after the results; none is statistically preferred.
PowerMinimum detectable average effect about 2.7% (80% power, 5% level); not detecting an effect does not show that there is none.
Maker casesEach CAR with a 95% interval of CAR ± 1.96 prediction-error standard deviations. No average is tested.

Estimands and the four weightings: paper, section 5.1; sample concentration: section 6.3.

10. Robustness checks

Prespecified 12 checks fixed before the results:

  • R1 portfolio t-test
  • R1 portfolio sign test (8 of 22 cluster CARs positive)
  • R2 plain t (ignores cross-correlation (literature baseline))
  • R3 rank (simulated p) (5000 simulations, seed 20261121; ignores cross-correlation)
  • R4 window [-1,+3] (unadjusted BMP t = 1.552; adjustment factor = 0.900; df = 21)
  • R5a drop low-confidence timing (unadjusted BMP t = 1.005; adjustment factor = 0.913; df = 20)
  • R5b low-confidence day 0 + 1 session (unadjusted BMP t = 1.193; adjustment factor = 0.917; df = 21)
  • R6 drop fallback-model events (unadjusted BMP t = 1.215; adjustment factor = 0.925; df = 20)
  • R7 drop multi-failure clusters (unadjusted BMP t = 0.987; adjustment factor = 0.934; df = 19)
  • R8 without AVIO.MI (unadjusted BMP t = 0.318; adjustment factor = 0.932; df = 13)
  • R8 without FLY (unadjusted BMP t = 1.123; adjustment factor = 0.945; df = 21)
  • R8 without RKLB (unadjusted BMP t = 0.686; adjustment factor = 0.952; df = 21)

Benchmark Placebo R9: 1000 sets of random ordinary trading days (seed 20261121), one per reaction. The real result's percentile is a description, not a p-value.

Exploratory Analyses designed after the results (confounding drops E1 to E3, weighting, a shared-date placebo R9b) are labelled wherever they appear and never replace the prespecified test. Results: paper, sections 7 and 8.

11. Limitations and data quality

Data quality
Announcement timestampsSourced company statements where available; otherwise GCAT launch times under the rule in section 5.
GCAT launch-time proxies37 of 42 rival reactions rely on them. Clock or time-zone errors beyond the allowed band would shift day 0.
Trading calendarsEach exchange's own calendar (Milan, Nasdaq, NYSE). A failure can fall on different calendar days for different rivals.
Confounding company newsNews around the largest reactions was checked against public sources (2 confounded, 1 sector move, 1 possibly confounded). Flagged reactions stay in the confirmatory test; dropping them is exploratory (E1 to E3).
Overlapping launch eventsFailures sharing a firm within 5 trading days are one cluster; check R7 drops multi-failure clusters.
Missing return dataFirm-events without a full window or estimation history are dropped and counted in the paper (Appendix E).
Thin trading and volatilityYoung listed firms are volatile; the standardized test scales each CAR by its own uncertainty, and fallback-model events are dropped in check R6.
SurvivorshipListed launch firms that later delisted (Orbital Sciences/Orbital ATK, Astra, Virgin Orbit) are absent from the free price source.
What is publishedOnly derived statistics are published: CARs, CAR variances, abnormal returns, residual volatility and CAR correlations. No raw prices, raw returns, alphas or betas.

12. Reproducibility

Python 3.11+, pip install -r requirements.txt, then run the steps in order. Prices are downloaded on your machine and never committed.

python pipeline/03_pull_prices.pyDownload prices (Yahoo Finance, not redistributed)
